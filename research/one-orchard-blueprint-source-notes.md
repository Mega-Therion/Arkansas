# Arkansas Orchard / ONE Natural Energy — Source Notes

Reference date: 2026-09-12. User brief is partly unavailable as an attachment in this sandbox; user-supplied facts are treated as assumptions until verified.

## Verified public sources

1. Entergy Arkansas Nuclear One facility profile: https://www.entergy.com/nuclear/arkansas-nuclear-one
   - Russellville, Arkansas; two PWRs.
   - Unit 1 maximum dependable capacity 836 MW; commercial operation Dec. 19, 1974; license expiration May 20, 2034.
   - Unit 2 maximum dependable capacity 988 MW; commercial operation Mar. 26, 1980; license expiration July 17, 2038.
   - Owner listed as Entergy Arkansas, LLC; approximately 1,048 full-time workers and 100+ baseline contractors.
   - Entergy says the units provide approximately 56% of energy demand of its 730,000 customers.

2. MISO Large Load Additions: https://www.misoenergy.org/planning/large-loads---container-page/large-load-additions/
   - MISO describes an unprecedented surge in large-load interconnection requests from data centers, advanced manufacturing, AI computing and energy-intensive industry.
   - MISO frames a three-part approach: connect quickly; transition and scale with flexibility; operate with long-term reliability.
   - Identified mechanisms include co-located resources/ZGIA, ERAS, expedited project review, non-firm service, firm-service step-up, telemetry, curtailment, ride-through, stability control, resilience and security.
   - MISO states large loads may seek 18–36 month connection timelines while transmission and generation can take longer.

3. Arkansas Public Service Commission electric page: https://apsc.arkansas.gov/utilities/electric/
   - APSC regulates 24 electric utilities, including investor-owned utilities and two RTOs.
   - APSC does not regulate municipally owned utilities, public power agencies or exempt wholesale generators.
   - Entergy Arkansas became a MISO member in December 2014.
   - New transmission facilities to connect generation generally require CCN or CECPN filings; tariffs govern rates, riders, rules and terms for jurisdictional utilities.

4. Arkansas Department of Agriculture: https://agriculture.arkansas.gov/
   - Search result states Arkansas has 37,400 farms and 13.7 million acres; agriculture contributes more than $24.3 billion annually. Verify exact current release before using in final as a hard number.

5. DOE nuclear/data-center context: https://www.energy.gov/ne/articles/advantages-and-challenges-nuclear-powered-data-centers
   - DOE discusses nuclear-powered data centers and the range of electricity needs, but this is context, not evidence of ANO commercial availability.

6. IRS clean electricity credits:
   - https://www.irs.gov/credits-deductions/clean-electricity-production-credit
   - https://www.irs.gov/credits-deductions/clean-electricity-investment-credit
   - Credits may be relevant to new qualifying generation/storage assets, subject to tax counsel, placed-in-service, prevailing wage/apprenticeship, domestic content, energy-community and foreign-entity restrictions. Do not assume they apply to nuclear output sold by an existing third party.

## Key underwriting discipline

- ANO is not assumed to be a captive power source. Any supply, co-location, wheeling, behind-the-meter, interruptible-load, or special-contract structure requires Entergy, MISO, APSC/FERC and NRC boundary review.
- ANO’s stated license expiration dates make 2034/2038 a material tenor constraint for any project whose economics depend on long-term nuclear adjacency or direct supply.
- The business should be separated into legally and operationally distinct SPVs: site/utility interface; flexible-load campus; energy services/DR; agriculture/food; and ecosystem/community entity.
- The initial financial model should use scenario ranges and milestone gates, not point forecasts, until capacity, tariff, interconnection, customer contracts, and land/water/permitting are documented.

## Unverified user-brief items to label as assumptions

- Exact full system description, orchard acreage, crop mix, irrigation/water rights, bioprocessing technologies, hydrogen/ammonia/thermal assets, load size and phasing, capex, customer pipeline, ownership/cap table, proposed tariff mechanics, and claimed business/impact metrics.
- Any claim that curtailed nuclear energy is available for capture, that ANO can serve a fence-line load behind the meter, or that a given NRC cyber boundary can be crossed by commercial controls.

# Arkansas Orchard: Offshoots, Connecting Membranes, and Unclaimed State Assets

**Prepared for:** R.W. Yett / Chyren  
**Date:** 12 September 2026  
**Status:** Research report; not a filing, feasibility study, legal opinion, or investment memorandum

## Executive conclusion

The Arkansas Orchard is strongest when treated as a **reusable operating grammar**, not as a fixed organization chart. The grammar has six recurring moves: put public or cooperative money in front of a cost; acquire liabilities before acquiring feedstock; capture value at the fence line of an energy or material source; use software to increase the yield of physical work; convert reentry and training into a labor pipeline; and preserve provenance so that public claims can be audited. The repository itself says that its branch structure is old and non-canonical, so the findings below are organized by mechanism rather than by assuming that the current six-branch chart is settled.[1]

The most defensible near-term extensions are not grand new industries. They are **interfaces**. ARMAWS can become a tire-safety and mobility service rather than only a free-air mandate. The driver-testing fleet can become a statewide access layer for licensing, workforce, and reentry. RENEW can become a credentialed remediation-to-employment system, but only if wage deductions, voluntariness, safety, and employer access are designed before revenue projections. refineRY should begin with characterization and recovery of demonstrably valuable streams rather than a broad claim that every Arkansas tailings pile contains economic rare earths. USGS data support gallium and niobium research in the central Arkansas bauxite district, but the same USGS work says the district's samples generally lack elevated critical-element concentrations, and it specifically finds that additional REE processing is unlikely to be economic in the foreseeable future.[2] [3]

The most non-obvious membrane is a **closed-loop mobility-and-materials corridor**: RENEW-trained workers and the state testing fleet feed licensed labor into ARMAWS service points; ARMAWS and highway cleanup create condition and location data; deliveRY moves recovered materials; recoveRY takes title or liability at documented sites; refineRY and factoRY turn selected outputs into products; memoRY provides repair, sensing, and edge compute; and ONE or cooperative generation supplies interruptible power where the economics actually work. This is more credible than treating AI compute as an independent cash engine.

The most promising resources not yet represented in the portfolio are **northwest Arkansas poultry litter**, **Arkansas rice residues**, **landfill gas and municipal organics**, **Arkansas River hydropower and ports**, and **rural water/broadband infrastructure as a service gap**. Each has a real Arkansas institutional or physical anchor. None should be presented as a ready business without site-level diligence, permitting, feedstock contracts, interconnection studies, and offtake proof.

## 1. Scope, evidence discipline, and claim tags

This report uses three claim tags where a proposition carries material analytical weight.

| Tag | Meaning | Use in this report |
|---|---|---|
| **[emp]** | Empirical or documentary claim supported by a cited source. | A documented Arkansas resource, program, law, study, or institutional fact. |
| **[conj]** | Conjecture or proposed extension. | A venture, bill, operating model, or connection that follows from the evidence but is not itself established. |
| **[thm]** | Mathematical or formally deductive claim. | Used sparingly; this report contains no new theorem. |

The private repository is a planning corpus, not an external authority. Its README explicitly marks several financial figures, job counts, tax benefits, recidivism savings, and branch structures as unverified or draft.[1] I therefore do not repeat those values as facts. The report also corrects an important overstatement risk: Arkansas bauxite is a serious subject for byproduct research, but “rare-earth-rich Arkansas bauxite” is not an established commercial fact. The USGS data release found that sampled bauxite and associated rocks generally lacked elevated gallium, REEs, and scandium, while the companion study found potential for gallium byproduct recovery, high niobium concentrations, and REE concentrations in residues that were probably not economic to process under the study's outlook.[2] [3]

## 2. Existing portfolio, translated into mechanisms

The current corpus describes foundRY as the holding and governance layer; ONE Natural Energy as a proposed fence-line energy and compute trunk; recoveRY as waste-site acquisition; refineRY/ACRN as hydrometallurgy and critical-material recovery; deliveRY as AI-assisted trucking and dynamic wireless power transfer; memoRY as hardware recycling and edge compute; libraRY as provenance and knowledge ledgering; factoRY as CNC, magnets, coils, pressure vessels, and alloys; and the civic layer as ARMAWS, driver-license testing reform, RENEW, and an Entergy ratepayer stabilization concept.[1]

The mechanisms are more durable than the names:

| Existing mechanism | Operational meaning | Arkansas test that must be passed |
|---|---|---|
| **State-pays-first** | Use an existing appropriation, surplus, fee, or public purchasing power to absorb the first cost. | The fund must be legally available for the proposed use, and the arrangement must not merely hide a recurring deficit. |
| **Liability-flip** | Take responsibility for an unwanted waste, site, or compliance burden in exchange for access, payment, or a value share. | Liability must be contractually bounded, environmentally permitted, and priced after characterization. |
| **Fence-line arbitrage** | Place conversion, storage, compute, or processing beside the resource source. | Interconnection, curtailment, safety, ownership, and rate treatment must be proven. |
| **Physical logistics plus optimization** | Use software to route people, vehicles, equipment, and recovered material. | Data rights, safety, labor acceptance, and measurable baseline savings are required. |
| **Labor-pipeline-as-payoff** | Make training, reentry, or public work produce portable credentials and a job transition. | Participation must be voluntary where required, paid lawfully, safe, and linked to real employers. |
| **Provenance ledgering** | Preserve chain of custody for material, labor, data, and public money. | Records must be accessible without exposing protected personal information. |

## 3. Offshoots from each existing piece

### 3.1 foundRY and the Diamond Foundation: convert governance into an operating product

**Offshoot: Arkansas Benefit-and-Liability Ledger. [conj]** Create a standard diligence instrument that follows each project from public input to environmental liability, labor credential, material output, and community return. The ledger would record who owns the site, who bears reclamation responsibility, what permits apply, what public funds are used, what workers are paid, what data are collected, and what claims are still uncertain. This is a natural extension of libraRY, but it should be mandatory inside foundRY rather than sold first as abstract knowledge management.

**Offshoot: staged-rights acquisition cooperative. [conj]** Extend recoveRY's proposed 90-day characterization, pilot, and commercialization sequence into a repeatable contract form. A site owner receives relief from an identified burden; foundRY receives a time-limited option; the operator earns a value share only after assay, permit, and sale. The contract should contain a walk-away right if characterization fails. This is the legal membrane that prevents “liability-flip” from becoming an unbounded environmental obligation.

**Bill idea: Public-Value Disclosure for State-Backed Demonstrations. [conj]** Any public demonstration receiving a state appropriation or fee diversion would publish a plain-language ledger of the subsidy, expected public benefit, actual performance, and residual liability. This is not a claim that Arkansas currently lacks all such reporting; it is a proposed common disclosure layer.

### 3.2 ONE Natural Energy: move from “AI campus” to flexible load and public-value infrastructure

Arkansas already has a substantial cooperative hydropower base. AECC reports three Arkansas River hydropower stations, approximately 193 MW of purchased hydroelectric energy, 4 MW of biomass, 474 MW of wind PPAs, and up to 27 MW of solar PPAs, together described as approximately 793 MW and more than 20% of its generation portfolio.[4] That fact makes a broader ONE thesis more plausible than an ANO-only thesis, but it does not prove that any particular plant has surplus power available for a new load.

**Offshoot: Interruptible Compute and Industrial Heat Exchange. [conj]** Instead of assuming that compute is always the highest-value load, ONE could bid a portfolio of interruptible loads: batch AI, storage, water treatment, industrial heating, refrigeration, and materials separation. The operating rule would be: use energy when the grid and contract permit; curtail on a pre-agreed signal; never characterize power as “stranded” until the utility, regional market, and generator owner confirm the constraint.

**Offshoot: Cooperative Resilience Nodes. [conj]** Co-locate containerized batteries, communications, water-pumping controls, and edge compute at selected cooperative substations. The public value is not merely compute revenue. It is islandable emergency capacity, telemetry, and local services. AECC's existing generation diversity makes cooperatives a more natural counterpart than an abstract statewide campus concept.[4]

**Bill idea: Flexible-Load Interconnection Standard. [conj]** Establish a transparent process for interruptible loads to interconnect behind or near generation, with required curtailment testing, customer protections, cost allocation, and public reporting. A statutory exemption from wheeling or congestion charges should not be presumed; it would require utility and regulatory analysis.

**Important correction.** The repository's references to “carbon-negative AI compute,” a 250-millisecond curtailment covenant, tax-credit yields, and job/tax totals are planning claims, not verified Arkansas outcomes.[1] A power project must first establish additionality, life-cycle emissions, interconnection, cooling water, cybersecurity, and rate impact.

### 3.3 recoveRY: become a site intelligence and environmental custody company

**Offshoot: Arkansas Brownfield Options Exchange. [conj]** Build a screened inventory of closed landfills, tire dumps, industrial parcels, mine residues, and contaminated sites. Each listing would include owner, regulatory status, known materials, groundwater constraints, access, nearby power, nearby transport, and a preliminary liability map. The product is not a marketplace for speculative minerals. It is a disciplined way to find sites where cleanup, reuse, and material recovery can be combined.

**Offshoot: Tire-to-road and tire-to-fuel separation service. [conj]** ARMAWS creates a statewide interface with tire retailers and motorists. recoveRY could use that interface to collect end-of-life tires, but only after confirming Arkansas's existing tire-fee rules, approved end uses, and the economics of rubber, steel, and fiber separation. The public justification is safer disposal and reduced illegal dumping; the commercial justification is feedstock aggregation.

**Bill idea: Closed-Site Characterization Fund. [conj]** Use an existing waste fee or recovered enforcement money to fund small, competitive characterization grants. The state would buy information first, not promise a mine or a cleanup contractor. Grant recipients would publish assays and risk data while preserving confidential business information.

### 3.4 refineRY/ACRN: narrow the first wedge to verified chemistry

The central Arkansas bauxite district is real and historically significant. USGS describes a district of about 275 square miles in Pulaski and Saline Counties, mined continuously from 1898 to 1982, and says the district produced an order of magnitude more bauxite and alumina than other U.S. districts combined.[2] [3] The same research reports average gallium of 76 ppm in raw ore, average niobium of 662 ppm in ore and 1,075 ppm in processed residues, low scandium, and REE concentrations highest in residues. It concludes that gallium is a potential byproduct and that additional REE processing was unlikely to be economic in the foreseeable future.[3]

**Offshoot: Gallium-niobium assay and process-validation service. [conj]** The first commercial product should be a paid characterization and process-development service for owners of residues, not a claim that refineRY already has a mineable REE deposit. The work would compare feedstock variability, recovery yield, reagent consumption, residue handling, and product purity. It can later inform factoRY products or a joint venture with an existing processor.

**Offshoot: Red-mud construction and ceramic materials. [conj]** If metal recovery fails the economic test, the residue may still have value as a controlled mineral input. This requires leachability, durability, and product-standard testing. The “failure” of an REE route could become a materials route, but that is a hypothesis, not a fact.

**Offshoot: Quartz quality registry. [conj]** Arkansas quartz should be treated as a quality-assurance and beneficiation question, not simply as “high-purity quartz.” A registry could combine deposit location, impurity profile, crystal quality, extraction constraints, and target use. Only samples that meet a defined specification should enter a semiconductor, solar, or crucible business case.

**Bill idea: Phase-Gated Residue Reuse Permit. [conj]** Create a permit pathway that allows sampling and pilot-scale recovery under a surety and monitoring plan before full commercialization. The state would get data and reclamation security; operators would avoid committing to a full industrial permit before chemistry is known.

### 3.5 deliveRY: extend from trucking into intermodal control

ARDOT defines intermodal planning as optimizing the state's transportation modes for passenger and freight movements and lists rail, public ports, waterways, and intermodal studies as active planning subjects.[5] AEDC describes more than 6,800 transportation, logistics, and distribution companies in Arkansas.[6] The opportunity is therefore not to invent Arkansas logistics, but to sell an intelligence and coordination layer into a large existing system.

**Offshoot: Materials Corridor Control Tower. [conj]** Route regulated loads from characterized sites to processors by combining truck, rail, and barge options. The control tower would optimize not only miles and fuel, but custody, assay integrity, driver hours, weather, road restrictions, and processor queue time. This is a more credible first use of AI than assuming DWPT corridors can be built statewide.

**Offshoot: Public test-fleet and employer shuttle network. [conj]** The driver's-license reform concept can share vehicles, maintenance, and scheduling infrastructure with rural workforce shuttles and RENEW transition transport. The legal and insurance design must keep testing, public transit, and commercial transport distinct even if they share depots.

**Bill idea: Rural Mobility Access Standard. [conj]** Require the state to measure the time and cost required for a person in each county to reach a license test, a workforce center, and a major medical or public-service hub. The first intervention can be administrative: mobile testing days, vehicle access, appointment coordination, and demand-responsive transit. A measurement obligation is safer than promising a new statewide fleet before utilization is known.

**DWPT caution.** Dynamic wireless power transfer is a capital-intensive infrastructure claim. It should be a later pilot on a fixed, high-volume, high-utilization segment, not an assumption embedded in every logistics model.

### 3.6 memoRY: make repair, reuse, and local compute the first business

**Offshoot: State and school device circularity. [conj]** Build a certified chain for state, school, hospital, and business equipment: secure data erasure, repair, parts harvesting, redeployment, component recovery, and only then material recycling. The differentiator is provenance and data security, not merely scrap value.

**Offshoot: Agricultural edge-service cooperative. [conj]** Place small compute and storage nodes at grain elevators, cooperatives, and substations where connectivity and power are adequate. Sell services such as equipment telemetry, local weather processing, machine-vision alerts, and store-and-forward connectivity. This aligns memoRY with rural logistics and agriculture without requiring a hyperscale data center.

**Bill idea: Public Hardware Reuse Preference. [conj]** Before state-owned computing hardware is auctioned or discarded, require a documented option for secure refurbishment and in-state redeployment, subject to cybersecurity and procurement rules. The policy should not force unsafe reuse or bypass existing surplus procedures.

### 3.7 libraRY and factoRY: turn knowledge and tooling into the quality layer

**Offshoot: Arkansas Process Passport. [conj]** Each recovered material lot would carry a machine-readable passport: site, date, assay, chain of custody, treatment history, contaminants, worker safety controls, and buyer specification. libraRY owns the provenance grammar; factoRY supplies sample fixtures, sensors, and small-batch tooling.

**Offshoot: Repairable public-works tooling. [conj]** factoRY could produce standardized, repairable fixtures and replacement parts for county road departments, water utilities, farms, and small manufacturers. This is less glamorous than magnets or pressure vessels, but it has a clear buyer problem: downtime and long lead times.

**Offshoot: Credentialed microfactory apprenticeships. [conj]** RENEW graduates and rural technical students could learn metrology, CNC, electrical safety, welding, and materials handling against actual production work. The ledger should record competencies and supervisor signoff, not merely hours attended.

### 3.8 ARMAWS: free air is the front door to a safety and maintenance network

**Offshoot: Tire Safety and Early-Warning Network. [conj]** A free-air requirement can be paired with calibrated gauges, voluntary anonymized counts of service use, referral to repair providers, and a clear prohibition against selling personal movement data. The policy goal is not to turn air pumps into surveillance devices. It is to measure whether access reduces unsafe underinflation and to locate service deserts.

**Offshoot: Rural service-point standard. [conj]** Where a retailer cannot host equipment, the state could certify alternative sites such as libraries, cooperative facilities, county yards, or mobile service vehicles. The criterion should be geographic availability and operating hours, not only the number of pumps.

**Bill idea: Safety Equipment Fee Transparency Act. [conj]** Require public accounting of tire-related fees, their permitted uses, fund balances, and grants. The repository's proposed use of a tire-recycling-fee surplus must be checked against the actual statutory dedication and available balance before drafting a bill.[1]

### 3.9 Driver-license reform: the fleet is an access platform

**Offshoot: Mobile Credentialing Fleet. [conj]** If the state can lawfully provide test vehicles, the same regional depots could support scheduled commercial-driver information sessions, identity-document assistance, and referral to workforce training. It must not collapse distinct licensing standards into one program.

**Offshoot: “No license, no job” barrier audit. [conj]** Use the fleet's appointment and geographic data to identify counties where the practical barrier is not the test vehicle but distance, insurance, documentation, internet access, or lack of instruction. This is a libraRY function: audit the actual failure mode before scaling.

**Legal caution.** The repository's assertion that the private-vehicle requirement is purely an administrative fossil should be verified against current Arkansas State Police rules and any statutory or policy materials before using it in public advocacy.[1]

### 3.10 Project RENEW: from labor program to accountable transition system

The Arkansas Department of Corrections defines reentry as a process beginning at incarceration and ending with successful community reintegration, and it frames stakeholder partnerships as a way to increase public safety, reduce recidivism, and slow prison growth.[7] That supports RENEW's direction, but not its inherited performance estimates.

**Offshoot: Reentry-to-credential compacts. [conj]** Build employer-specific pathways for environmental services, CDL support, materials sorting, electrical maintenance, CNC, and data-center physical security. Each compact would state the credential, wage, supervisor, transport plan, background-check rule, and first-90-day support. A promise of “guaranteed employment” should be reserved for employers that sign actual conditional offers.

**Offshoot: Reentry asset escrow with independent fiduciary control. [conj]** The escrow idea should be redesigned around participant ownership, transparent deductions, grievance rights, and independent auditing. The repository's 40/30/30 split and projected exit balances are not verified and must not be treated as a legal template.[1]

**Bill idea: Remediation Work Rights and Safety Act. [conj]** Establish voluntary participation, wage and safety requirements, medical and heat protections, access to grievance procedures, and a ban on conditioning basic rights on labor participation. If prison-industry rules or federal certification apply, counsel must map the limits before any program begins.

### 3.11 Entergy rate stabilization: add a data and benefit-allocation layer

The ratepayer proposal should not be presented as a simple subsidy. Its unresolved question is who bears costs, who receives the benefit, and whether a surplus-funded stabilization account reduces volatility or merely delays a rate increase.[1]

**Offshoot: Rate Impact and Benefit Ledger. [conj]** Require public separation of generation cost, fuel adjustment, capital recovery, transmission, demand growth, and large-load contributions. If ONE or any data center benefits from fence-line access, the ledger should show whether residential customers receive a measurable offset, whether the load is interruptible, and who pays for grid reinforcement.

**Offshoot: Community energy-dividend pilot. [conj]** A defined portion of verified incremental revenue from a large flexible load could fund weatherization, emergency power, or bill credits in the affected service area. This is not a claim that such a dividend is currently lawful or economically sufficient.

## 4. Connecting membranes: non-obvious links between existing pieces

### 4.1 RENEW → refineRY/factoRY: the chemistry-to-tooling labor loop

This is the most direct unused connection. refineRY needs sample preparation, safe chemical handling, equipment maintenance, solids handling, quality control, and environmental monitoring. factoRY needs machinists, welders, metrology technicians, and maintenance workers. RENEW can feed both only if the program trains to recognized competencies and employers commit to actual work. The conjecture is not “incarcerated labor solves advanced manufacturing.” The defensible proposition is narrower: a staged, paid, voluntary training pipeline can reduce entry barriers for jobs that are difficult to fill, provided the jobs exist and safety is non-negotiable.[conj]

### 4.2 ARMAWS → recoveRY: the tire network as a reverse-logistics map

A statewide free-air network creates a distributed set of service points. Those points can also become voluntary collection or referral nodes for damaged tires, but only with clear separation between safety service and waste handling. Aggregate service geography could identify slow-leak and tire-replacement deserts. That data could guide mobile repair, tire reuse, or compliant recycling. The link is operational, not rhetorical: **the same rural locations that need air may be the locations where tire waste is least well served**.[conj]

### 4.3 Driver-license fleet → deliveRY → RENEW: access before employability

The driver-testing fleet addresses a prerequisite that RENEW's employment bridge may otherwise assume away. A person can complete training and still fail to reach a job because they cannot obtain a license, insure a vehicle, or travel to the test. A shared regional depot can coordinate testing, instruction, employer transport, and post-release appointments. This is a three-layer intervention: administrative access, physical mobility, and employment.[conj]

### 4.4 ONE → memoRY → libraRY: compute should be a service to physical operations

The credible connection between power and edge compute is not “nuclear electricity automatically creates an AI campus.” It is that flexible local compute can reduce latency and connectivity requirements for material tracking, equipment monitoring, route optimization, and emergency operations. ONE supplies a possible energy input; memoRY supplies hardware and local deployment; libraRY supplies provenance and data governance. The combined product is a **trusted industrial data service**, not a speculative compute-arbitrage number.[conj]

### 4.5 refineRY → deliveRY → factoRY: assay-aware logistics and modular process equipment

Material recovery fails economically when feedstock variability, queue time, contamination, and equipment downtime are ignored. deliveRY can route lots according to assay and processor availability. factoRY can build modular sampling, filtration, pumping, and handling equipment. libraRY can preserve lot identity. The membrane is a “digital thread” from site to product. It is non-obvious because it makes logistics and provenance part of the refinery, rather than overhead after the chemistry is finished.[conj]

### 4.6 RENEW → ARMAWS → public-health prevention

Highway cleanup, tire-dump remediation, free air, and mobility access can be treated as one prevention chain: remove hazards, reduce unsafe vehicle operation, and create paid transition work. The connection should not be sold with unverified claims about fatality reduction. It should be tested with baseline measures: service-point availability, tire condition referrals, cleanup tonnage, repeat dumping, worker injuries, and employment outcomes.[conj]

### 4.7 Rate Stabilization → ONE: the benefit-allocation contract

The civic and commercial energy layers are coupled whether the portfolio acknowledges it or not. If a fence-line load receives special treatment while households bear plant or grid costs, the arrangement will be politically and substantively unstable. The membrane is a public-benefit contract: verified interruptibility, transparent cost allocation, local resilience investment, and a defined ratepayer protection mechanism. The point is not to guarantee that a data center lowers rates; it is to make the claim falsifiable.[conj]

## 5. Other Arkansas resources and civic gaps not yet in the portfolio

### 5.1 Northwest Arkansas poultry litter: a real liability with a research base

The Arkansas Agricultural Experiment Station reports approximately 1.3 million metric tons of poultry litter annually, concentrated in northwest Arkansas. It states that repeated land application can produce excessive plant-available phosphorus and that transport out of the watershed only temporarily alleviates the nutrient burden.[8] The same research program is developing a liquid-state anaerobic digestion system intended to produce biogas and struvite fertilizer, with water recycling and pathogen reduction as part of the design.[8]

**Opportunity: litter-to-energy-and-nutrient hubs. [conj]** A new branch or recoveRY extension could aggregate litter and compatible organic wastes, produce biogas for on-site heat or flexible generation, and recover struvite. The correct first step is not statewide deployment. It is a contracted pilot with one integrator or cluster, a verified feedstock radius, nutrient-of-take agreements, odor controls, and a measurement plan comparing the digester route with existing fertilizer transport.

**Membrane to the Orchard:** ONE supplies the fence-line energy logic; recoveRY supplies liability transfer; deliveRY supplies aggregation; factoRY supplies modular handling; RENEW supplies training; libraRY records nutrient and emissions data. The risk is that existing fertilizer use already captures some value, so the project must prove incremental economics and environmental benefit.

### 5.2 Arkansas rice residues: a fiber, silica, and logistics opportunity

The Arkansas Department of Agriculture describes Arkansas as the leading U.S. rice-producing state and reports that the sector supports a substantial research and promotion apparatus.[9] Rice hulls and straw are therefore not a generic agricultural residue; they are a concentrated local material stream.

**Opportunity: rice-hull ash and fiber materials platform. [conj]** Candidate products include controlled silica-rich ash, absorbents, construction additives, packaging fiber, or process heat. The first diligence question is not whether rice hulls contain silica; it is whether a specific mill can supply consistent material, whether combustion or processing meets air rules, and whether the resulting product meets a buyer specification. This could fit refineRY and factoRY better than a broad “biofuel” claim.

**Membrane:** rice logistics can share deliveRY routing and memoRY edge nodes at mills, while waste heat or biogas could support local processing. RENEW crews could not be assumed into industrial processing without a separate safety and workforce design.

### 5.3 Landfill gas and municipal organics: the missing mature waste-to-energy interface

Arkansas environmental materials recognize that municipal solid waste decomposition produces methane and that landfill gas is a regulated air-quality issue.[10] This is a more mature and site-specific resource than an uncharacterized mineral tailings theory.

**Opportunity: landfill-gas and organics optimization service. [conj]** recoveRY could acquire or contract for underperforming gas systems, while ONE supplies flexible load and memoRY supplies sensors and controls. The public contract would be methane capture and compliance; the commercial revenue would be energy, renewable attributes where legally available, and avoided operating cost. The project must distinguish existing permitted landfill-gas obligations from genuinely incremental capture.

### 5.4 Arkansas River hydropower, ports, and industrial corridors

AECC documents three Arkansas River hydropower stations and a long history of cooperative investment in low-head, run-of-river generation.[4] ARDOT and the Arkansas Waterways Commission treat rail, ports, barges, and trucks as parts of an intermodal system rather than isolated assets.[5] [11]

**Opportunity: powered intermodal materials corridors. [conj]** Instead of building DWPT first, use existing river and rail infrastructure to move dense, non-time-critical materials such as recovered minerals, steel, aggregates, fertilizer, or processed biomass. Add energy-aware scheduling, barge-to-rail transfer, and verified lot custody. The commercial wedge is software, handling, and selected terminal improvements; the civic wedge is better use of existing infrastructure.

**Bill idea: Intermodal Brownfield Reuse Program. [conj]** Prioritize state support for terminal, rail-spur, and industrial-site improvements that remove a documented bottleneck and include environmental, labor, and public-access metrics.

### 5.5 Rural water and broadband: infrastructure as a service gap

Arkansas's infrastructure programs explicitly cover broadband, transportation, water, and energy, and the state's BEAD materials frame the objective as reaching unserved and underserved locations.[12] These are not merely connectivity issues. They are prerequisites for telemetry, telehealth, remote training, agricultural automation, and distributed operations.

**Opportunity: rural utility telemetry and cyber-resilience service. [conj]** memoRY and libraRY could provide low-cost, locally managed monitoring for small water systems, substations, and broadband facilities. ONE's energy layer could add battery-backed communications at critical nodes. The public buyer is a utility or county; the value is fewer blind spots, faster fault detection, and an auditable maintenance record. This is not a claim that the portfolio should become a general IT contractor. It is a targeted application of edge compute and provenance to public infrastructure.

**Bill idea: Critical Utility Data Minimums. [conj]** Establish grant conditions requiring asset inventories, incident reporting, backup communications, and maintenance records for publicly funded rural water and broadband systems. The state should fund the compliance burden for small systems rather than imposing an unfunded mandate.

### 5.6 Forest residues and biochar: use the state’s biomass geography carefully

The Arkansas Department of Agriculture identifies forest resources as a state responsibility, and research has examined woody residuals as a potential biomass resource.[13] Existing private activity also indicates that Arkansas forestry residues can be transformed into engineered carbon materials, but one company is not proof of an available statewide market.[14]

**Opportunity: distributed forest-residue preprocessing. [conj]** Use small, mobile preprocessing units to reduce hauling of low-density residues, then sell chips, heat, biochar, or engineered carbon feedstock to known buyers. This fits the liability-flip only where slash or residues impose a real cost or hazard. It fits fence-line arbitrage where a sawmill, industrial heat user, or cooperative generator has a confirmed offtake.

### 5.7 Occupational-licensing implementation: a low-capital civic venture

The University of Central Arkansas policy analysis describes Arkansas as licensing 71% of a sample of 102 occupations, compared with a 53% national average, and discusses universal license recognition as a mobility reform.[15] The repository separately records a Legislative Council finding that some Agriculture boards accepted zero licenses under the recognition framework, but that specific claim should be rechecked against the original attachment before publication.[16]

**Opportunity: licensing compliance ledger. [conj]** libraRY could build a public, non-personal dashboard showing application dates, statutory deadlines, recognition pathway, board response, and appeal route. This is a rule-vs-law audit made operational. It does not require a new licensing regime. It requires clean records, an escalation path, and a way to distinguish lawful denial from administrative delay.

### 5.8 Reentry and employer transport: turn the existing institutional goal into a measurable service

The Arkansas Department of Corrections states that reentry is a process and identifies community engagement and stakeholder partnerships as central to reducing recidivism and slowing prison growth.[7]

**Opportunity: first-90-days transport guarantee. [conj]** A limited pilot could guarantee transport, identification appointments, license testing, and employer arrival for the first 90 days after release. It would test the proposition that employment failure is partly a mobility and coordination failure. This is a better first experiment than promising a statewide employment percentage.

## 6. Prioritization: what to test first

| Rank | Candidate | Why it is first-order | Minimum proof before scaling |
|---:|---|---|---|
| 1 | Poultry-litter biogas and struvite pilot | Real concentrated feedstock; Arkansas university prototype; clear nutrient problem. | Signed feedstock and offtake contracts; mass balance; permitting; net energy and nutrient economics. |
| 2 | Process-passport and site-characterization service | Uses libraRY, recoveRY, refineRY, and deliveRY without requiring a new mega-project. | One paying site; repeatable assay and custody workflow; defined liability boundary. |
| 3 | Reentry-to-credential compact | Strong fit with DOC's stated partnership model and the portfolio's labor-pipeline logic. | Voluntary participation; wage/safety review; employer commitments; independent outcome measurement. |
| 4 | Public hardware circularity and rural edge nodes | Clear integration of memoRY, libraRY, and ONE; avoids hyperscale assumptions. | Secure-erasure standard; hardware supply; one utility/agriculture customer; measured uptime and savings. |
| 5 | Arkansas River powered intermodal corridor | Builds on documented hydropower and intermodal assets. | One corridor, one commodity, one terminal, verified comparative delivered cost. |
| 6 | Bauxite residue gallium/niobium characterization | Strong scientific basis but uncertain economics; valuable as disciplined R&D. | Site-specific samples; recovery yields; reagent and residue economics; buyer specification. |
| 7 | ARMAWS service-point network | Low physical complexity and visible civic value. | Statutory fee review; equipment cost; rural coverage map; complaint and safety metrics. |
| 8 | Statewide DWPT corridor or hyperscale compute campus | Potentially transformative but capital-, regulatory-, and grid-intensive. | Utility studies, anchor customer, corridor utilization, safety certification, and ratepayer analysis. |

## 7. Claims that should not be carried forward without verification

The following should remain explicitly unverified or be removed from public-facing material until sourced and audited: projected ONE job counts and tax benefits; the stated 45U per-MW figure and its applicability to the exact structure; carbon-negative compute; the repository's RENEW recidivism, employment, savings, and scrap-revenue estimates; the 40/30/30 escrow split; the claim that ARDOT's litter cost and piece counts are as stated; the use of an existing tire-fee surplus for ARMAWS; the legal characterization of the driver-test vehicle rule; the 2027 sponsor and pre-filing commitments; and any claim that Arkansas bauxite residues are commercially rich in REEs.[1] [2] [3]

The report's core negative finding is constructive: **the portfolio does not need inflated numbers to become more coherent**. It needs a small number of falsifiable pilots, clean public ledgers, and contracts that allocate environmental, labor, ratepayer, and data risks before upside is advertised.

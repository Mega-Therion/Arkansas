# The six "–RY" branches

| Branch | What it does |
|---|---|
| **recoveRY** | Waste-site acquisition |
| **refineRY / ACRN** | Hydrometallurgy on legacy mine tailings, bauxite, and coal-ash; high-purity quartz (semiconductors/solar) and critical battery-mineral extraction (lithium, manganese, cobalt) via $0-upfront phase-gated rights |
| **deliveRY** | Bilocal 1:3–8 AI trucking, coupled with Dynamic Wireless Power Transfer (DWPT) resonant-corridor infrastructure |
| **memoRY** | Used-hardware Bitcoin/storage → rare-earth-element recycle; containerized edge compute nodes co-located at agricultural substations and grain elevators (DePIN — decentralized physical edge AI infrastructure) |
| **libraRY** | Knowledge and playbooks; sovereign second-brain knowledge ledgering with tamper-evident provenance |
| **factoRY** | Modular CNC precision tooling, pressure vessels, inductive coils, magnets and alloys |

Every branch feeds back into foundRY's roots, which feeds the community — wealth stays in Arkansas
instead of draining to an out-of-state or coastal conglomerate.

## Statutory pillars supporting the branches

**Ouachita Mineral Sovereignty & Tailings Reclamation Code** (model act, supports recoveRY/refineRY):
- Prohibits debt-loaded land encumbrance — outlaws predatory mineral leases that encumber surface
  owner deeds or impose upfront development liabilities.
- Statutory royalty floor: surface landowners get a mandatory gross-revenue royalty from refined
  critical-mineral sales (lithium carbonate/hydroxide, electronic-grade quartz, battery manganese).
- Closed-loop tailings surety: operators must deposit reclamation collateral into escrow before
  breaking ground on legacy tailings or bauxite remediation.

**Arkansas Resonant Freight Transit Infrastructure Act** (model act, supports deliveRY):
- Grants state highway/county authorities the mechanism to create public-private DWPT corridors
  connecting mining/tailings sites to refinery hubs.
- In-road inductive coils energize only on cryptographic verification of an approaching licensed
  hauler — no ambient radiation, no wasted energy.

**Rural Computational Dividend (RCD)** (model ordinance):
- 5% of net computational-arbitrage revenue generated in any county deposits into a County Public
  Works & Energy Resilience Trust.
- Restricted to rural substation upgrades, community emergency solar/battery micro-hubs, and
  vocational apprenticeships in CNC machining and high-voltage electrical engineering.

Status: statutory framework/blueprint stage, not yet filed.

## refineRY/ACRN — high-purity quartz feasibility (honest, still open)

RY specifically asked to chase this angle hard, so here's the real state of it as of 2026-09-11 —
not oversold.

- **The real prize:** 70–90% of the world's semiconductor-grade high-purity quartz (crucibles for
  growing silicon ingots) comes from just two mines in Spruce Pine, NC, controlled by two foreign
  companies (Sibelco/Belgian, The Quartz Corp/Norwegian) — a genuine, well-documented chokepoint
  with **zero US export controls**.
- **Arkansas's real position — not yet confirmed either way:** Mount Ida is genuinely "the Quartz
  Crystal Capital of the World," and USGS documents it as high-purity feedstock for growing
  *synthetic* quartz crystal (piezoelectric/oscillator use) — a **different market** than
  crucible-grade fused quartz, which needs an extremely tight trace-aluminum spec. No public
  ppm-level assay data was found confirming or ruling out Arkansas quartz for crucible grade.
  USGS Bulletin 973-E (Engel, 1952) is the primary historical source but its geochemical tables
  weren't reachable via search — would need the actual document.
- **No one appears to be working this angle:** no company, patent, or DOE/DOD critical-minerals
  program was found evaluating Arkansas quartz for electronics/semiconductor processing — all
  found activity is specimen/jewelry trade (Ron Coleman Mining / Coleman Quartz). Quartz/silica is
  also **absent from both the federal and Arkansas critical-minerals lists**, so the federal
  funding angle that applies to the REEs above doesn't extend to quartz.
- **Live and current:** the Forest Service is deciding by August 2026 whether to lease 347 acres in
  the Ouachita National Forest (Garland/Saline counties) for new quartz mining — public comment
  closed May 5, 2026, no applicant publicly named yet, opposed over a projected 825% sedimentation
  increase into Lake Winona (Little Rock's drinking water source). A separate, unrelated Ouachita
  quartz project (Mena Ranger District) was cancelled — don't conflate the two.
- **The one thing that would actually settle this:** real trace-aluminum assay data on Arkansas
  quartz. Nobody appears to have published it. That's the concrete next step, not a business plan.

## refineRY/ACRN — the market case (why now)

- **Price volatility as the opportunity:** neodymium normally runs $60–80/kg but spiked to
  **$160/kg** in 2022; dysprosium normally $300–400/kg spiked to **$700/kg** in the same window —
  REE supply shocks are real and recent, not theoretical.
- **The supply-chain vulnerability this exploits:** China controls roughly **60% of REE mining**
  and **85–90% of refining** capacity globally.
- **Federal money already chasing this exact gap:** the DOE is offering **$134M** specifically for
  domestic REE production, aimed at reducing China dependence — a live funding target, not a
  hypothetical.
- **RecoverRY's acquisition protocol**, more concretely than "waste-site acquisition" above:
  **Phase 1** — a 90-day characterization pilot on a candidate site; **Phase 2** — pilot
  operations; **Phase 3** — commercialization, with value-sharing to the site owner only realized
  at the point of sale (the "$0-upfront" model above, made concrete).
- **Named target feedstocks:** coal ash, bromine-rich brines (lithium), REE-rich mining tailings —
  alongside the bauxite residues already documented in
  [`one-natural-energy.md`](one-natural-energy.md).

## The liability-flip framing

Tailings and waste sites are **environmental liabilities to their current owners**, which inverts
the usual economics: rather than recoveRY paying for feedstock, a site owner may pay recoveRY to
take the liability off their hands. This is the mechanism underneath "$0-upfront" above, stated
plainly.

# The Diamond Foundation

The citizen-facing half of the portfolio's governing philosophy (the system-facing half is the
Gentle Authority Doctrine — see [`../commercial/foundry-and-governance.md`](../commercial/foundry-and-governance.md)).

## Origin

Arkansas is home to the only public diamond mine in the world — Crater of Diamonds, Murfreesboro.
Walk in, dig, keep what you find. The foundation isn't a borrowed metaphor; it's named after the
thing the state is literally standing on. Earlier working name was "Glass Foundation" — renamed
because glass implies something you see *through* but not *into*, can shatter, can be frosted,
can hide behind while still being called transparent. A diamond doesn't shatter, and every facet
is a different angle on the same structure — completely legible if you know how to look.

> The state does not hand you the diamond. The state makes sure the ground is open, the tools
> work, and the claim is yours. That is ARMAWS. That is the license fleet. That is the power plan.
> It starts with us.

## The principle

**Radical legibility:** clear, un-obfuscated statutory text that any citizen — named examples:
Mountain Pine, Little Rock — can inspect and understand, with perfect clarity, like looking
into a diamond rather than through a glass wall.

## Where each initiative sits under it

| Initiative | The cage | The state's role | The fix | Funding source |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **ARMAWS** | Paywall on safety-critical air | Regulates fuel retailers | Free air, signage, enforcement | Tire recycling fee surplus |
| **License Fleet** | Private-vehicle requirement to test | Administers the test | State test cars, rotating fleet | Surplus and forfeited vehicles |
| **Power Plan** | Monopoly with surprise bills | Approved the monopoly and rates | Rate Stabilization Fund | General revenue surplus |
| **ONE Orchard** | Stranded baseload, wealth drain | — | Fence-line compute, closed-loop ecosystem | Utility revenue share, IP portfolio |
| **Project RENEW** | Recidivism trap, gate-money poverty | — | Paid cleanup, escrow savings, job pipeline | Offset from reduced prison costs |

Same money philosophy across all five: zero new taxes, existing surplus, deterministic cost. Same
transparency rule: any citizen can see the structure. Same compact: **security without
dependency**.

## The motto

**"Make no prisoners."** Not the old pirate-code inversion (leave nobody standing) — the target is
the cage itself, not a person. The power monopoly makes prisoners of every meter. The license trap
makes prisoners of anyone who can't afford a car to take the test. The recidivism cycle makes
prisoners of people released with nothing. The air paywall makes prisoners of anyone who skips a
tire fill because it's two dollars too much that week. Four initiatives, one sentence: stop making
prisoners.

## The overlap principle

Drawn from comparing pirate-ship articles (principle, no standards) against naval regulations
(standards, no principles): the rules that show up in *both* — no gambling with the ship's money,
no stealing from shipmates, no drinking below deck, always keep your weapons clean — aren't about
authority. They're survival rules for a closed environment where one person's failure endangers
the person next to them. The Diamond Foundation initiatives are framed as belonging in that
overlap: not partisan rules, "ship rules" — free air, a test car, a bill that doesn't surprise you,
a person leaving prison with savings and a job.

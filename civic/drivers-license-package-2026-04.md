## Document 2: Executive Summary
**The Problem**
Arkansas requires noncommercial driver's license applicants to complete a road skills test. Under current administrative policy, applicants must supply their own registered, insured vehicle in good mechanical condition to take the exam. Arkansas has embedded a private-asset prerequisite into a public licensing process.
**The Legal Gap**
Ark. Code § 27-16-704 requires a skills examination, and § 27-16-705 gives the Arkansas State Police (ASP) the authority to administer it. Neither statute requires the applicant to supply the testing vehicle. The private-vehicle barrier is an administrative creation (ASP Driver's Testing 1-2-3 Skills Checklist, revised June 24, 2025) unsupported by statutory mandate.
**Core Principle**
If Arkansas requires a road test, Arkansas must provide at least one non-means-tested public pathway to take it without owning a compliant private vehicle. We don't discriminate, we elevate — and it starts with the people at the bottom.
**The Ask — Fiscal Session 2026**
 * **Special Language:** Insert special language into DPS/ASP and DFA appropriations directing the design and costing of a 5–10 vehicle pilot program.
 * **Budget Oversight:** Utilize budget hearings to clarify ASP/DFA legal authority, fleet options, and insurance frameworks on the record.
 * **Interim Study:** File an interim-study proposal with the Bureau of Legislative Research (BLR) for the House Public Transportation and Senate Transportation, Technology & Legislative Affairs committees.
**The Ask — Regular Session 2027**
 * **Statutory Amendment:** Amend Ark. Code §§ 27-16-704 and 27-16-705 to codify a public access guarantee to testing vehicles.
 * **Fleet Authorization:** Explicitly authorize ASP and DFA to procure, own, and operate a statewide testing fleet (~40 vehicles total).
**Pilot Scale & Vision**
The initial pilot requires 5–10 vehicles deployed to highest-need counties (e.g., Troop K / Montgomery County). The long-term target is a ~40-vehicle statewide fleet, rotated based on regional need, ensuring testing access for all Arkansans regardless of vehicle ownership.
## Document 3: Full Legal and Control Memo
**Statutory Framework**
The core legal foundation for driver testing in Arkansas rests on two statutes:
 * **Ark. Code § 27-16-704:** Mandates a skills examination, specifically an "actual demonstration of ability to exercise ordinary and reasonable control in the operation of a motor vehicle." (Arkansas Code, 2025).
 * **Ark. Code § 27-16-705:** Designates the Arkansas State Police (ASP) or duly authorized agents of the Department of Finance and Administration (DFA) as the administrators of the exam. It grants ASP the authority to promulgate "any necessary rules" to implement examinations. (Arkansas Code, 2025).
**The Administrative Policy Gap**
Nowhere in Title 27 is the applicant legally required to supply the vehicle. The requirement is entirely derived from ASP operational policy, specifically the *ASP Driver's Testing 1-2-3 Skills Checklist (revised June 24, 2025)*. This policy dictates that no private vehicle means no test.
**Agency Jurisdiction & Control**
 * **ASP / DPS:** Controls examinations, rulemaking for the exam, testing site locations, and scheduling by regional troop.
 * **DFA / ODS:** Controls licensing issuance and manages state vehicle procurement via the Motor Vehicle Acquisition Revolving Fund (Ark. Code §§ 22-8-201–211; § 19-5-1002). DFA already possesses Act 1006 "Purchase of Vehicles" authority, supporting up to $15 million in existing vehicle purchases.
**Necessity of Statutory Action**
 * *For the Pilot (2026):* No new statute is required. ASP and DFA possess the existing legal and procurement authority to launch a pilot. Special appropriation language and agency directives are sufficient to operationalize a 5-10 vehicle test phase.
 * *For the Permanent Guarantee (2027):* A statute *is* necessary to prevent future administrative backsliding. A statutory access guarantee ensures the private-vehicle prerequisite cannot be quietly reinstated via internal ASP rule changes.
**Quorum Court Authority**
County quorum courts lack the jurisdictional authority to alter state driver-license requirements or ASP testing procedures. However, they possess the power to pass nonbinding advisory resolutions to formally express county-level legislative intent and pressure state actors.
## Document 4: Legislative Path Analysis
**Fiscal Session 2026 Strategy: The Wedge**
The 2026 session is constitutionally restricted primarily to appropriations. Substantive general bills amending Title 27 are inadmissible. Therefore, the immediate strategy relies on fiscal mechanisms:
 1. **Special Language:** Draft special language for inclusion in the DPS/ASP and DFA appropriation bills. This language will direct the agencies to use existing Act 1006 vehicle purchase authority to design, cost, and report on a 5-10 vehicle pilot.
 2. **Budget Hearings:** Coordinate with Rep. Maddox and Sen. Rice to use Joint Budget Committee (JBC) hearings to compel ASP and DFA directors to publicly address the asset-test barrier and confirm their existing authority to procure fleet vehicles.
 3. **Interim Study Proposal:** File an interim study formally moving the issue into BLR's queue for review by House Public Transportation and Senate Transportation, Technology & Legislative Affairs between sessions.
**Regular Session 2027 Strategy: The Root Fix**
The 2027 regular session permits substantive statutory changes.
 1. **Statutory Access Guarantee:** Introduce a general bill amending §§ 27-16-704 and 27-16-705 to explicitly forbid ASP from making private vehicle ownership a prerequisite for a noncommercial road skills test.
 2. **Explicit Fleet Authorization:** Statutorily authorize DFA/ASP to maintain a statewide testing fleet (~40 vehicles).

## Document 8: Draft Legislative Findings
**(1)** The State of Arkansas requires applicants for a noncommercial Class D driver's license to successfully pass a road skills examination to demonstrate safe operation of a motor vehicle.
**(2)** Current administrative practices require applicants to supply their own registered, insured, and mechanically sound private vehicle to participate in the mandatory public examination.
**(3)** This requirement embeds a private-asset prerequisite into a public licensing process, creating a disproportionate barrier for low-income and rural Arkansans.
**(4)** A driver's license is a fundamental requirement for full economic and civic participation, necessary for securing employment, accessing healthcare, and supporting families, particularly in rural counties lacking public transit infrastructure.
**(5)** The state possesses a compelling economic and public safety interest in ensuring all capable drivers are properly tested, licensed, and insured, rather than driven to unlicensed operation out of necessity.
**(6)** Other jurisdictions, including the District of Columbia, have successfully implemented public test-vehicle fleets, proving the operational feasibility of state-provided testing access.
**(7)** Removing this barrier aligns with the state's commitment to self-sufficiency, workforce development, and equitable access to public services without reliance on arbitrary means testing.
## Document 9: Bill Concept Memo
**Working Title:** The Driver's License Public Access Guarantee Act of 2027
**Statutory Targets:** Ark. Code Ann. §§ 27-16-704 and 27-16-705
**Objectives:** To eliminate the private-vehicle prerequisite for driver testing and authorize a state-managed test fleet.
**Key Provisions:**
 1. Amends § 27-16-704 to state that no applicant shall be denied a skills exam solely due to the inability to provide a private vehicle.
 2. Amends § 27-16-705 to authorize ASP and DFA to procure, manage, and insure a fleet of official vehicles dedicated exclusively for skills testing.
 3. Prohibits the use of means-testing, hardship applications, or additional fees to access state testing vehicles.
   **Appropriation Components:** Companion appropriation of $250,000 for the first biennium to fund capital acquisition and operation of a Phase 2 fleet size (10-15 vehicles).
   **Political Positioning:** Framed as a workforce development and rural self-sufficiency measure. It removes an artificial government barrier to employment.

## Document 12: Pilot Framework Memo
**Troop K Anchor Pilot Mechanics**
 * **Deployment:** 2-3 vehicles assigned to Troop K, prioritizing Montgomery and Hot Spring county schedules.
 * **Reservation System:** The existing online/phone ASP scheduling system will add a checkbox: "State Vehicle Requested."
 * **Access Rules:** Universal access. No means testing. No extra fees. Vehicles are for test-access only, driven only during the 15-minute exam.
 * **Control:** Keys remain with the ASP examiner at all times. Vehicles are parked at secure ASP or county law enforcement lots (e.g., Montgomery County Sheriff's Office lot) overnight.
 * **Insurance:** Vehicles are added to the existing state fleet risk management policy. Accidents occurring during testing are handled under standard state vehicle incident protocols.
 * **Reporting:** ASP to track data on usage rates, cancellation reductions, and maintenance costs, submitting a 12-month pilot report to the House Public Transportation Committee and JBC.

## Document 18: Illustrative Applicant Scenarios
**Scenario A: The Workforce Gate**
A single mother in Montgomery County receives a job offer in Mount Ida, but it requires a valid driver's license. Her family vehicle recently suffered a blown transmission and is off the road. Under current rules, even though she is a capable driver and has a permit, she cannot take the test because her vehicle does not meet the mechanical checklist. She loses the job opportunity due to a temporary lack of a physical asset, despite being fully prepared for the skills exam.
**Scenario B: The Milestone Block**
An 18-year-old high school senior in rural Arkansas has completed driver's education and holds a valid permit. The only vehicle in their household is a 15-year-old truck with a cracked windshield and a faulty taillight—automatic disqualifiers under the ASP 1-2-3 checklist. Because they cannot borrow a pristine vehicle, they cannot take the test. A standard rite of passage and marker of adult independence is blocked by an administrative asset test.

## Document 20: Anticipated Objections and Targeted Rebuttals
**1. "We can't afford another state fleet."**
*Rebuttal:* A 5-10 vehicle pilot costs $130k max—0.035% of the FY25 surplus. At full 40-vehicle statewide rollout, capital costs are ~$1M. Arkansas manages a $6.6B general revenue budget. This is a negligible investment for immense workforce returns.
**2. "People should have a car before getting a license."**
*Rebuttal:* This is a catch-22. You cannot legally test-drive or purchase a car easily without a license. It artificially blocks young adults and low-income workers from taking the first step toward self-sufficiency.
**3. "This will be abused as a free rental-car program."**
*Rebuttal:* Impossible by design. The vehicle keys never leave the ASP examiner's possession. The vehicle is strictly used for the 15-minute exam under state supervision.
**4. "We don't want to be in the insurance business."**
*Rebuttal:* The state already is. Arkansas insures thousands of state vehicles. Adding 10 Chevy Traxes to the existing DFA risk management policy is an administrative routine, not a new business venture.
**5. "Rural areas won't have enough volume to justify this."**
*Rebuttal:* Rural areas have the *highest* need because they have zero public transit alternatives. The pilot deploys based on need-rank, ensuring vehicles go where the asset-barrier is most severe.
**6. "This will grow into a big budget line."**
*Rebuttal:* The fleet is capped by the statutory authorization. A rotating fleet of 40 vehicles covers the state efficiently; there is no operational justification for it to expand beyond that fixed logistical ceiling.
**7. "What about equity across other counties/troops?"**
*Rebuttal:* We solve for equity by deploying the pilot to the objectively highest-need counties first (determined by poverty rates and lack of local sites), and aggressively phasing into a statewide guarantee by 2027.

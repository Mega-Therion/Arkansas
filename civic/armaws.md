ARMAWS guarantees every Arkansas traveler a free source of mechanically pressurized air to fill their tires. Its frame is **tire safety as a public utility**: "you don't means-test a headlight."

## The problem: "the Tire Tax"

Gas stations put air behind coin or card paywalls ($0.75–$3.00 a cycle), so low-income and rural drivers put off filling slow leaks. The cascade from an underinflated tire:
- excessive sidewall flex and heat buildup;
- high-speed delamination and rollover;
- longer stopping distances;
- an unbudgeted crisis: a $200 tow, a $150 tire, a missed shift.

**The person it's written for (RY, 2026-06-21, verbatim):**

> "two bald tires and a plug in the third, while the fourth has a slow leak they have to wait until payday to afford to patch, so they're putting air in every other day just to keep getting to work."

A few dollars of air against a lost job, a $200 tow or a highway blowout. That asymmetry is the whole moral and economic case.

## Statutory precedent

| Jurisdiction | Statute | Model | Outcome |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Connecticut** | Conn. Gen. Stat. § 14-325a (P.A. 79-260, 1979; amended 1988, 2004) | Free air to **anyone**, no purchase required; ≥ 80 psi; conspicuous sign | Durable for about 47 years. **The ARMAWS template.** § 14-325a verified 2026-10-03 |
| **California** | Bus. & Prof. Code § 13651 | Free air **and water**, but tied to a fuel purchase | Widely evaded: machines switched off, receipts demanded. The failure mode to avoid. *(Characterization not re-verified.)* |
| **Florida** | Turnpike service plazas only | No station mandate | Leaves secondary roads unserved |

**Research finding (2026-06-21):** these laws don't fail at the vote. They fail through **non-enforcement, low public awareness and the purchase-condition loophole**. Primary roll calls and testimony haven't been pulled yet.

**Expected opposition, so the bill can answer it ahead of time:**
- cost of electricity and maintenance;
- free-rider abuse;
- trade-association resistance from fuel retailers;
- takings arguments (compelling private machines without paying for them).

## Design decisions

1. **Unconditional access, the Connecticut rule.** No purchase is required. A purchase condition creates friction between customer and clerk and makes enforcement impossible.
2. **Air only (locked 2026-06-21).** Water drags in plumbing, backflow, winter freezing, contamination and runoff law. Connecticut's air-only law has lasted 47 years.
3. **The "Three Teeth":**
   - mandatory pump-island signage;
   - a designated enforcement owner and complaint line: the **Department of Agriculture's Bureau of Standards (Weights & Measures)**, Ark. Code Ann. § 4-18-301 et seq. ✓;
   - a tiered civil penalty (a warning first, then escalating fines).
4. **The Rural Mobility Air Fund and takings protection.**
   - 100% equipment-retrofit grants for independent retailers.
   - Safe-harbor immunity while a retailer waits for equipment.
   - This is what defuses the takings challenge: the state pays for what it requires.

## Funding: no new tax

Existing used-tire fee revenue under the **Used Tire Recycling and Accountability Act**, Ark. Code Ann. § 8-9-401 et seq. ✓ (Act 317 of 2017; ADEQ Tire Accountability Program; PC&E Rule 36), plus general-revenue surplus. FY2026 ended with a **$655M general-revenue surplus** (DFA, July 2026 ✓).

**Open item:** the claim that tire-fee revenue *can* legally be redirected hasn't been checked against the fund's statutory dedication or its actual balance. Check both before drafting a funding section that names that fund.

## Sponsor strategy (2026-06-21)

Don't treat all legislators as one group. There are three:
1. **Group 1:** members who have personally aired up a slow-leaking tire to make a shift. They feel it.
2. **Group 2:** members who never had to. They hear it as a stranger's story.
3. **Group 3:** the persuadable middle.

**Group 1 is the messenger, not the audience.** A Group-1 colleague telling the floor "I have done this; this is overdue" moves Group 2 in a way an outside citizen can't. So the play is to **recruit a Group-1 prime sponsor and arm them**.

Ways to find them:
- rural and Delta districts;
- first careers (mechanic, trucker, factory, service work);
- votes on cost-of-living bills;
- seats on the transportation, public-utility and agency-budget committees.

The public pitch is **recognition, never reproach**: "this is yours to carry; you already know why."

## Extensions (from the 2026-09-12 offshoots report; `[conj]`)

- **Tire Safety & Early-Warning Network.** Calibrated gauges, voluntary anonymized use counts, and referral to repair shops, with an explicit **ban on selling movement data**.
- **Rural service-point standard.** Where no retailer exists, certify libraries, co-op facilities, county yards or a mobile unit.
- **Safety Equipment Fee Transparency Act.** Public accounting of every tire-related fee, its permitted uses and its balance.
- **Orchard membrane.** The service-point map becomes recoveRY's tire-collection geography (see [`../SYNTHESIS.md`](../SYNTHESIS.md)).

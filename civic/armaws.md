# ARMAWS — Arkansas Rural Mobility Air Standard

**Classification:** Public Safety & Rural Mobility Infrastructure
**Status:** `BILL_DRAFT_A_v1.md` exists — furthest along of the civic initiatives
**Target:** pre-filing November 2026, for Arkansas's 2027 Regular Legislative Session

Guarantees every Arkansas traveler a free source of mechanically pressurized air to fill their
tires.

## The problem — "the Tire Tax"

Coin/card-operated air paywalls ($0.75–$3.00) at gas stations force low-income and rural drivers
to postpone filling slow leaks. Underinflation → excessive sidewall flex and heat buildup →
high-speed blowouts, fatal rollovers, longer stopping distances, and catastrophic unbudgeted
expense ($200 tow, a missed shift).

The working-class reality the bill is written for: *"Two bald tires, a plug in the third, and a
slow leak waiting on payday."* A few dollars of air against a lost job, a missed shift, a $200
tow, or a highway blowout — that asymmetry is the entire moral and economic case.

## 50-year statutory analysis

- **The gold standard — Connecticut §14-325a** (in force since 1979): mandates free air to
  anyone, unconditionally, no fuel purchase required, with conspicuous pump-island signage,
  ≥80 psi. ~47 years durable, near-universal compliance.
- **The failure mode to avoid — California B&P §13651 / AB 531** (eff. Jan 2000): conditioned free
  air on a fuel purchase, creating an under-enforced loophole — stations turn off machines or
  demand a paper receipt. Widely ignored.
- **Air only, deliberately** — water was dropped from scope to avoid clean-water/plumbing/freeze/
  EPA-runoff liability. This is a scope decision, not an oversight.

## The "Three Teeth" + takings protection

1. **Mandatory signage** — clear pump-island signage notifying motorists air is free.
2. **Designated enforcement owner** — Arkansas Department of Agriculture (Weights & Measures) /
   ASP complaint hotline, escalating tiered fines.
3. **The Rural Mobility Air Fund** — 100% state-funded equipment-retrofit grants for independent
   mom-and-pop retailers, funded via the existing state tire-recycling-fee surplus. Retailers get
   safe-harbor immunity while awaiting state retrofit support — this is what removes the takings
   risk of compelling private machines without funding the cost.

## Funding

Not a new tax — a reallocation of the existing tire-recycling-fee surplus.

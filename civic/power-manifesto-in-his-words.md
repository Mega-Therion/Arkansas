# "thank God i live in Arkansas": the power manifesto, in RY's words

**Provenance:** On 2026-08-18 RY asked an AI agent (Notion) to "pull all my word for words and assemble them into a narrative … and create a manifesto on my stance on Arkansas power situation and what my plan is." This is that page, copied unchanged from Notion (page "⚡ thank God i live in Arkansas", last edited 2026-08-18). It is built from his own messages in a ChatGPT thread, "Electric Bill Price Factors"; the agent only bridged the gaps between them. His lowercase style is kept as written.

---

this is not a policy paper. this is what i actually mean.

in Arkansas, people look up and their electric bills have jumped all at the same time, out of nowhere, regardless of a change in their usual energy consumption. that is the thing i want to put a stop to. not the lights. not the work. the flexible rate. the part where everybody gets charged extra because a line got crossed somewhere they never agreed to watch.

for me it is Entergy. i do not need a choice of twenty providers to see the problem. i need a state that will not let a bill become a surprise tax on living here.

there is no reason the state of Arkansas could not put even five dollars in an account for every person in whatever territory. even if that is all the extra cost estimation comes out to, that is nothing for a state with a surplus like ours. the state is not a bunch of thems. our state is made up of all of us.

there should be a hard cap on what any customer pays for electricity. everyone pays the same base price for energy. if you use more quantity than your neighbor, your bill is higher only because you used more energy. that is it. that is the only honest reason a bill should be bigger.

there is a line. whatever situation it is that makes the company decide they need more money from customers, that is the line. right now, when that line is crossed, they charge everyone extra. that is the wrong shape. the people who used less did not cause that line to be crossed. they should never pay extra for a crossing they did not create.

this is not a voucher from your neighbor to you. you do not need to know what the house over there is doing with their power. we are all each others neighbors. all you know, as a customer, is that you pay a set rate for your energy, just like they do.

if there is ever a need to ask more money of you or your neighbor for any reason, Arkansas steps up first. the state has a set budget allocated just for occasions like this, resources meant to be there for taking care of its citizens. those allocated amounts sit on a rollover. if nine months are perfect and three months are rough, those three months have nine other months of budget to take the load off.

only after that allocated money is depleted, and only if more is still needed, is the remainder split among the customer base. worst case, the low power user pays a minuscule amount extra, and the average consumer pays a minuscule amount, and even that is only after Arkansas has already paid what it set aside.

and the system should narrow its own margins before any single tax paying Arkansas citizen is asked to individually take on the extra collective burden. if seven houses in an area only used seventy five percent of their average monthly energy, those people do not get treated like they owe twenty five percent more. they do not pay that extra. that unused twenty five percent is discounted from the people who used more than their average. the system eats its own slack first. even if the extra burden was caused by an isolated event, the citizen is not the first wallet they reach for.

this is not about who has power and who is in charge. it is about doing the right thing for the right things sake. one thing any leader should keep in mind is that no matter how many people you are responsible for, what you might be ignorantly calling power is actually just a platform. and if you elevate yourself too high on such a platform... well you know what they say. the further you rise.

if i were in charge i would not be worried about how high my platform can rise. i would be focused on how to expand it and add steps so everyone can walk up on the platform and we can all rise together, and nobody ever has to be under our platform or our power.

if the governor is considered any more special than any other citizen, it is only because those same citizens consider them that way for how close and concerned they are, and for what they have done for them. people keep the saying backwards. do not start with what our citizens can do for our state. start with what our state can do for our citizens, so that realistically they would not need to be concerned about doing it themselves.

my state should empower me to walk forward on my own, for my own. and where it cant, i know it has my back.

that is the whole electric plan in one sentence. a hard, honest rate for the power you used. a state surplus standing in front of the surprise. a rollover so the good months carry the rough ones. a system that spends its own unused margin before it spends a citizens. and only then, if it is still not enough, a minuscule shared remainder.

people should not wake up thinking about Entergy, or the Public Service Commission, or what the government is doing to them this month. they should just wake up and think, thank God, i live in Arkansas.

if you are doing something right, people wont know youre doing anything at all. that is close, but it is a dangerous line. invisible is not the right word. invisible implies accepting a structure you can see and touch and grasp without ever looking inside it. unseen is worse. unseen means you may notice something is there, but you do not understand it, and you are afraid of it.

the right word is transparent. there is a structure you can tangibly grasp. you can understand why it is transparent. you are not afraid of it because you can see it inside and out and watch it operate and know the structure is there.

so that is the stance. Arkansas electricity should be a set rate for what you use, a hard cap against the surprise, a state that pays first out of money it already set aside for its people, a rollover so one bad season cannot become a statewide shock, and a transparent structure any citizen can watch without needing to be a lawyer or a lobbyist. the company can still run. the lights can still stay on. the citizen just does not get treated like the emergency fund.

the state does not walk your path for you. it makes sure the road is not collapsing under you. then it gets out of the way and lets you build.

that is how you do the right thing for the right things sake. that is how nobody has to live under the platform. that is how a person in Arkansas can walk forward on their own, and still know, where they cant, the state has their back.

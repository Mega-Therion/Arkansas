# DRAFT — Public Comment for APSC Docket No. 26-001-U (Entergy Arkansas Rate Case)

**Status:** DRAFT — not filed with the Commission. Prepared by R.W. Yett, Arkansas ratepayer.
**Purpose:** For use by any elected official, staffer, or advocate working this docket. Free to use, in whole or in part.
**Companion:** the mechanism behind the ask is drafted at `civic/drafts/rate-stabilization-fund-model-bill.md`.
**Hearings:** public comment October 20 and October 27, 2026 (per Commission Order No. 15, read 2026-10-13); evidentiary hearing November 4–5, 2026.

## What's on the table (figures verified 2026-10-14 against docket filings and public sources)

- Entergy proposes moving the residential fixed **customer charge from $8.40 to $12.92/month** (~54% jump) — proposed, not final.
- Entergy proposes rolling **~$650 million/year in rider revenue** into base rates (Arkansas Business, 8/31/2026: "about $650 million in annual rider revenue built up incrementally over the years").
- Entergy cites a ~$44.6M revenue deficiency and claims <2% overall impact (~$1.16/mo for a 1,000 kWh residential customer); AAEA's independent math says **4.9% for residential customers** — the discrepancy is on the record.
- New rates would take effect **January 2027** if approved.
- Entergy's offset: a Low-Income Qualified Discount Rider (50% off the customer charge for 113,000+ LIHEAP households), recovered by raising other residential components ~0.84% for non-eligible customers (per AAEA's read of the Klucher testimony).

## The position

**Arkansas is Entergy's golden goose.** Arkansas Nuclear One — 1,824 megawatts of fully amortized, carbon-free baseload — is the goose that lays for them. The LA/MS/TX ratepayers (the gander) deserve the same protection.

I don't oppose recovery of real costs. I oppose turning temporary rider spikes into permanent base-rate increases. Once a rider is in the base rate, it never comes back out.

## The ask

1. **Reject the rider-to-base-rate roll-in** in 26-001-U.
2. Adopt a **Rate Stabilization Fund — state pays first — keyed to the fuel adjustment clause.** Analysis of 2010–2026 Arkansas prices shows a *generic* fund doesn't smooth anything: it merely delays increases by up to a year while 86–92% of its dollars chase rises still present two years later. Keyed to fuel adjustment, the fund absorbs the shocks instead of postponing them. (Model bill text: `civic/drafts/rate-stabilization-fund-model-bill.md`.)

**To be fair to the record:** Entergy's own reported average-impact number is small. The plan's argument isn't that this one case is huge. Its argument is about **shape**:
- a fixed charge that rises whatever you use, which hits low users hardest;
- riders that move every meter at once;
- temporary riders becoming permanent base rates.

- **Commission General Staff** (Doc. 225): Entergy has excess revenue of almost $79M, about $123M away from what it requested.

## How a comment gets in

The Commission's online form for this docket is at `apps.apsc.arkansas.gov/olsv2/PublicCommentForm.asp?DocNum=26-001-U` (opened 2026-10-13 ✓). Comments can also go by mail to the Secretary of the Commission, in person at the Commission's office, or orally at a public comment hearing.

Two things the form states that matter before anyone files:
- It requires a name, mailing address, city, state, postal code and e-mail address.
- "Your public comment will be posted on the Commission's website and will be available for viewing by any member of the public." The form page does not say which of the required fields are published with it.
- What a posted comment shows: the docket's public listing (228 comments on 2026-10-13) gives each commenter's name and the date. One posted comment opened that day, submitted through the online form, showed the commenter's name, mailing address, e-mail address and IP address in the public PDF. That is one sample, not a survey; assume everything typed into the form is published.

## The four things that move a bill

| Mechanism | What it does | Who controls it |
|---|---|---|
| Base rate + fixed customer charge | Per-kWh price plus a flat monthly charge | APSC, in a rate case |
| Fuel adjustment clause | Passes fuel and purchased-power costs straight through | APSC review; automatic pass-through |
| Formula rate plan | Annual true-ups without a full case | APSC approves the formula; the math runs |
| **Generating Arkansas Jobs Act rider** | Lets a utility recover the cost of a new plant **during construction** (construction work in progress, CWIP) | **Act 373 of 2025** (SB 307, approved 3/20/25; defines "construction work in progress") ✓ read in the act text; APSC implements it |

RY's name for these four: **"the four hands in the pocket."**

APSC rate authority: Ark. Code Ann. § 23-4-101 et seq. ✓. The standard is "just and reasonable" rates.

## The fix: the Arkansas Rate Stabilization Fund

From RY's own rules (see the manifesto):

| RY's rule | What it means in the machine |
|---|---|
| Same base price for everyone | A bill rises only with the kWh you use, not with a surprise rider |
| Hard cap | No uncapped pass-through to households |
| **The state pays first** | A surplus-funded rollover account absorbs the increase before any household surcharge |
| The system eats its own slack | Unused low-usage margin offsets high-usage residuals before anything is split |
| Only then a split | Any remainder is shared, and citizens are the last wallet, not the first |
| Transparent | Every dollar published in plain numbers |

**Funding:** general-revenue surplus. FY2026 closed with **$655M** ✓ (transferred to the General Revenue Allotment Reserve Fund). It's already collected and already Arkansan, so there's no new tax. FY2026 total reserves were reported at about $4B.

## Four moves, fastest to deepest

1. **A public comment on 26-001-U.**
   - Don't fold riders into base without a sunset.
   - Don't raise the fixed charge on low users.
   - If the state wants new plants, the state stands in front of the rider.
   - It's free. As of 2026-10-13 a comment can still go in through the Commission's online form, by mail, in person, or orally at the October 20 and October 27 public comment hearings. The parties' issues list or settlement is due October 23, and the evidentiary hearing is November 4–5. See "How a comment gets in" above for what the form requires and publishes.
2. **A Rate Stabilization Fund statute.** This is the mechanism; see the model bill.
3. **A citizen-initiated constitutional amendment.**
   - Signatures equal to 10% of votes cast in the last governor's race, gathered in at least 15 counties *(per the 2026-08 session; re-verify the exact requirements)*.
   - Draft language from that session: *"Where the State of Arkansas does not grant a retail choice of electric provider, the State shall first apply already-collected general revenue to absorb extraordinary residential rate adjustments before any such adjustment is billed to a citizen."*
4. **Build the public record.**
   - Who intervened in the docket.
   - Public filings on how Act 373 was passed.
   - Published **as a map, not an accusation**.

The **constitutional arguments** raised in the session (takings under Ark. Const. art. 2 § 22, and substantive due process) are **`[O]` legal theories, not findings**. An Arkansas utility lawyer has to test them before anyone uses them publicly.

## Messaging discipline (RY's strategy, 2026-08)

RY sets the targeting rule: the problem is **institutions, not individuals**. In his words:

> "what my goal is to do is to draw attention to the fact that the carnival game is what is rigged, it has nothing to do with the game operator taking your money. its the fact that we as a state have accepted a rigged carnival game as a real fair game for everyone to play fairly."

- **No individual is named.** Deliberately.
- **The thesis:** the state has accepted a rigged carnival game as a fair game.
- **Opening move: the mirror, not the answer.** "Look at what we have become," posed as a rhetorical question.
- **The plan is ready first.** The bills, the fund and the Diamond Foundation are complete *before* the mirror goes up, so people who look and don't like what they see have somewhere to go.
- **The work walks on its own legs.** It goes to whatever leaders emerge, not to a single-founder campaign. RY's framing is "the leader nobody knows about," Batman as a principle, not a person.

## How it fits the other bills

There are three traps of the same shape: a paywall on air, a vehicle requirement on a test, a monopoly on power. In each, the state set the condition, and the citizen pays for being stuck in it.
- ARMAWS and the license fleet are **small, fast and cheap**. They prove the state can absorb a cost without a new tax.
- The power plan is then "the third thing the same philosophy produced."

## Extensions (`[conj]`)

- **Rate Impact and Benefit Ledger.**
  - A public breakdown of generation, fuel, capital recovery, transmission and large-load contributions.
  - If ONE or any data center gets fence-line access, the ledger shows whether residential customers get a measurable offset.
- **Community energy dividend.**
  - A share of verified revenue from a large flexible load funds weatherization and bill credits in the affected area.
  - This isn't a claim that such a dividend is currently lawful.
- **"Four Streams into One Living Water"** (parked 2026-10-03). Also called the **goose/gander play**.
  - A four-state ratepayer compact across Entergy's territories: Arkansas, Louisiana, Mississippi and Texas.
  - RY's framing when he parked it: fold the other three states into the same fix, "to try to play the whats good for the goose is good for the gander sort of angle."
  - The parked outline had three parts: surplus-funded rate buffers before each state's commission, coordinated interruptible industrial load, and the four territories acting as one ratepayer bloc.
  - Nothing further on it is in this repository.
